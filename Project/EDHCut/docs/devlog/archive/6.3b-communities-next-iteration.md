# Task 6.3-B — Communities: color bias, granularity, and Girvan-Newman
Date: 2026-08-12 | Status: **archived — abandoned 2026-08-13 at the user's direction**

> Superseded by [6.3c-louvain-communities.md](../6.3c-louvain-communities.md). The communities
> were too large, the colour-bias problem this task existed to solve was never solved, and the
> four bespoke modules it introduced were not understandable enough to reason about. Kept for the
> report's "what we tried and abandoned" material. Note that 6.3-D later revived
> `symnmf_packages.fit_symnmf` on the *gated* graph, where it behaves differently.

Three questions from live review of the 6.3 notebook: can the colour-identity fix be used with
soft clustering; can 40 topics become meaningfully more granular; is Girvan-Newman relevant.

## Decisions
- **SymNMF over the color-conditioned t-score matrix, not a patch to raw-presence NMF.** A card's
  colour legality is *structural* in deck×card — a mono-green card has literally zero support
  outside green-legal decks at any column scaling, and non-negativity forbids subtracting an
  expectation. Per-column rescaling is mathematically incapable of removing block structure;
  frozen "colour background" components would need a custom update loop anyway.
- **Custom damped multiplicative-update solver** (`β=0.5`), never forming `H@Hᵀ` (~2.7 GiB dense
  at n≈18,000) — everything routes through `k×k` intermediates. `β` was checked live across 18
  (k, seed) combinations after the claim was written unverified: undamped `β=1` genuinely
  oscillated in 2 runs (65 of 149 iterations with a rising residual), `β=0.5` in none.
- **Basics-only land exclusion**, not all-lands — `nmf_packages`'s all-lands rule fixed a
  raw-presence-specific failure mode that t-score doesn't share. Re-verified live rather than
  assumed by analogy.
- **Recursion, not a wider flat sweep, for granularity** (`symnmf_hierarchy.py`): fit a small
  top-level split, induce `S` on each topic's own members, split again, stopping on size, depth,
  or a child stability floor. The tree is fit once; `cut_at(n_leaves)` makes any granularity level
  a cheap lookup. Deliberately not a strict partition — a card can recurse down several branches.
- **`theme_labels.py` was built as the measuring stick *before* any new clustering**, with
  "resolved" (`min(share_of_topic, recall_of_label)`) kept distinct from "covered" — conflating
  them would have made granularity look solved when it wasn't. It reproduced the planning
  session's hand-computed baseline to the last digit before being trusted.
- **Step 4 (anchored/semi-supervised SymNMF) was evaluated and not built** — the plan made it
  conditional on granularity saturating below target, and it didn't. Not attempted by decision.

## Bugs
- **A units mismatch in the color-conditioned null model**, found before it could bite. `joint`
  supports deck weighting but `N_i` (`eligible_deck_count`) counted raw decks, so under
  near-uniform weighting — where `total_weight` ≈9,000 diverges from 13,207 raw decks — `joint`
  scaled and `expected'` didn't, systematically under-correcting. Harmless for every prior caller;
  this task was the first to combine the two. `build_color_identity_deck_counts` gained a
  `deck_slot_weights` parameter, verified by an integration check that its weighted sum matches
  `build_cooccurrence(...).total_weight` exactly.
- **`granularity_report` silently returned 0.0** for any non-contiguous `topic_id` scheme — it
  assumed `0..k-1`, true for every flat build but not for a hierarchy cut, whose ids are node ids.
  Caught by noticing a whole column was flatly 0.0.
- **`cut_at` renormalized a card's share across nodes from *different local fits***, whose weights
  share no scale — summing them isn't a measurement, it's noise. Cultivate legitimately clears
  threshold in 44 of 1,200 frontier nodes, and the renormalization diluted all 44 below threshold
  at once, so the card vanished from the output entirely. Caught by looking up a named example in
  the notebook and getting zero rows where five were expected.
- Both slipped past review *and* the test suite because the toy fixtures were too small to trigger
  them — "the tests pass" and "ran it against real data and checked a specific number" are
  different levels of assurance.
- Backgrounded Python is block-buffered when piped, so long diagnostics looked hung for tens of
  minutes while progressing normally. Use `python -u` / `flush=True`, and don't read "no output"
  as evidence of a hang.

## Outcome
Abandoned, but three results are worth keeping:

- **Q1, colour bias — a genuine negative result, reported rather than patched around.** Target was
  median purity ≤45%; measured 68.3% against flat NMF's 66.7%, essentially unchanged. Investigated
  rather than accepted: sampling 500 cards' top-50 neighbours shows same-colour share dropping
  48.3% → 44.7%, so the correction *is* working at the edge level — it just doesn't propagate to
  topic purity. Best explanation: much of what looked like colour bias was never a legality
  artifact but genuine archetype structure (Goblins really is red), which a null-model correction
  neither can nor should remove. Pushing harder to hit the pre-declared number would be
  over-correcting past what the data supports.
- **Q2, granularity — both targets met once the sweep was widened.** The plan's ~400-leaf ceiling
  was conservative; resolved-label count peaks at **356 around 1,200 leaves**, and median
  labels-per-topic crosses ≤6 around 1,700, so both hold simultaneously from ~1,700 on. Fit cost
  was also far below the plan's estimate (k=300 in 143s, not a week) because `S` is a much sparser
  problem than deck×card.
- **Q3, Girvan-Newman — ruled out, and the plan's escape hatch measured false.** Globally
  infeasible (~5.9×10¹⁵ ops) and a hard partition besides. The assumed "cheap at one-topic scale"
  claim didn't hold: `community_edge_betweenness` on one real topic took **65 minutes**.
  Restricting to a topic's top-150 by weight runs in 10–14s but selects for hub cards, so the best
  cut is one 84-card cluster plus 66 singletons. The useful by-product was direct observation of
  **modularity's resolution limit**: fastgreedy's modularity is flat from ~16 clusters out to
  1,000 while the largest cluster barely shrinks — empirical confirmation of why any flat
  objective tops out short of real granularity, and why recursion was the right lever.
