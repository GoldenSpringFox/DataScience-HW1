## Goal & hypothesis

User-directed reset. The 6.3-B soft-clustering line was abandoned on three stated grounds: the
communities were too large, the colour-bias problem it existed to solve was still unsolved, and the
tooling (NMF, symmetric NMF, recursive hierarchy cuts, bespoke theme labelling) was not
understandable enough to reason about. The request was to go back to basics with Louvain — taught
in the user's course, hard partition, recursive levels, visible modularity — and to produce one
interactive 2D graph where cards are points, edges appear above a threshold, and tightly-connected
clusters sit near each other.

Hypothesis: the simpler method would not do worse. It did substantially better.

## Data

Unchanged corpus: 13,207 decks / 1,001 commander slots / 18,979 cards in `card_index`.

**Deck weighting is the load-bearing data decision.** The corpus is lopsided — Krenko 2,007 decks
(15.2%), Kyler 1,235 (9.4%), Yenna 350, Yoshimaru+Bruse Tarl 300, against a median slot of ~8.
`compute_near_uniform_weights` (`median(n_s)/n_s`) gives a Krenko deck weight 0.0040 against a
median slot's 1.0; total weight 8,006 vs. 13,205 raw decks.

Graph after `sparsify_top_k_union(top_k=15, min_tscore=0)` on the weighted colour-conditioned
t-score, basics excluded: **18,381 nodes, 223,979 edges**, degree median 16 / max 4,294, edge
t-score median 2.24 / p90 4.34 / max 32.8. 598 cards dropped for having no surviving positive edge.

### The regression that had to be found first

The notebook initially loaded the saved `tscore_color_global.npz`. **Every saved matrix under
`data/kb/dev/` is unweighted** — `cooccurrence.build_and_save` never passes `deck_slot_weights`,
and has no parameter to. `communities.build_and_save` already works around this by rebuilding its
own weighted matrix (its docstring names Krenko/Kyler/Yenna as the reason); the new notebook did
not, and silently regressed the fix.

This was **caught by the user from the picture** — visible giant Krenko and Kyler blobs — before
any metric flagged it. Measured effect once corrected:

| | unweighted | near-uniform weighted |
|---|---|---|
| community holding Krenko's 29 signature cards | 1,026 cards, captures 93.1% | 487 cards, 75.9% |
| community holding Kyler's 36 signature cards | 1,068 cards, captures 83.3% | 326 cards, 41.7% |
| largest community | 1,087 | 868 (at γ=2) |
| package-sized (8–60) | 105 | 114 |
| modularity Q | 0.699 | 0.576 |

The Q drop is the expected cost, not a loss: the oversampled blocks were dense and trivially
separable, so they were inflating modularity. §"Guard" below is now a permanent notebook section so
this cannot regress silently again.

**Metric design note, recorded because the first attempt was wrong.** "Card appears in ≥1 Krenko
deck" is vacuous at 2,007 decks — 2,900 cards qualify, mostly generic red staples, and it reported
95% domination at every γ including builds that were fine. The measure that works is a card
appearing in **≥50% of that slot's decks** (Krenko: 29 cards, Kyler: 36, Yenna: 28), then asking
what share of that signature set lands in a single community.

## Method & design decisions

**networkx over igraph**, reversing task 6.3. `louvain_partitions` yields *every level* of the
recursive merge from one run, which is the granularity ladder for free; igraph's
`community_multilevel` only exposes this via `return_levels`. The user's course taught networkx.
3.5s on the full 18k graph, so performance was not a reason to prefer igraph. `igraph`/`leidenalg`
remain project dependencies (`communities.py` still uses them) but the notebook uses neither.

**ipysigma over pyvis/plotly** for the graph. It takes a networkx graph directly and runs
ForceAtlas2 in the browser on WebGL — edge weight becomes attraction, which is exactly the "strong
clusters near each other" requirement, with no precomputed layout. pyvis (vis.js) is the other
common answer and was rejected on scale: it realistically tops out ~1–2k nodes against 18k here.
Plotly would have required precomputing a layout in Python and hand-drawing edge traces.
Verified live: renders 18k nodes; `write_html` works but emits ~50 MB files (embeds the renderer).

**γ = 2.0, not 1.0.** Measured on the weighted graph: at γ=1 level 0 has 188 communities with the
largest at 2,298; at γ=2 it has 214 with the largest at 868 and 114 package-sized. The weighted
graph is less blocky than the unweighted one and wants a higher resolution.

**Level 0, not the final level.** A sweep of the *final* partition across γ ∈ [0.5, 5] never
produced more than 24 package-sized communities even at γ=5 (103 communities, Q=0.54). Level 0 of
a single γ=2 run gives 114. The level hierarchy is the granularity knob; γ is secondary.

**Colour bias: measured, then accepted.** Louvain on the plain vs. colour-conditioned matrix gives
the same purity (52.9% vs 52.1% median, null ≈17–20%) — a third method agreeing with 6.3 and 6.3-B
that the colour signal is in the data, not in any algorithm. Decision taken explicitly with the
user: stop trying to remove it, measure and report it per community, and read the right tail as
where colour and mechanic genuinely coincide. The colour-conditioned matrix is still used, on a
different justification: it splits better (195 vs 166 communities, largest 1,087 vs 1,532).

## Evaluation & results

Criteria fixed before looking: communities should be package-sized and read as recognizable MTG
archetypes; no single commander should dominate one; the picture should be legible.

**Final build** (weighted, colour-conditioned, γ=2, seed 42):

| level | communities | Q | largest | median | package-sized (8–60) | singletons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **214** | 0.576 | 868 | 39 | **114** | 0 |
| 1 | 57 | 0.612 | 1,348 | 210 | 8 | 0 |
| 2 | 55 | 0.612 | 1,348 | 215 | 7 | 0 |

Colour purity: median 52.8% across 190 communities ≥8 cards, against a 20.0% same-size random null.
Slot guard: Krenko 72.4%, Kyler 38.9%, Yenna 64.3%.

**The sanity check passes.** Level-0 communities read as real packages — Extra Turns (Time Warp,
Temporal Mastery, Expropriate, Nexus of Fate), Shrines (Sanctum of Stone Fangs, Honden of Infinite
Rage, Go-Shintai), Cascade (Apex Devastator, Imoti, Bloodbraid Elf, Shardless Agent), Allies (Hada
Freeblade, Kabira Evangel, Kazandu Blademaster), Necrons, Deserts, Equipment, Landfall. The 40 NMF
topics never did this.

### Automatic community naming (works, and its failures are diagnostic)

`card_tags` (Scryfall Tagger, 377k rows, ~950 tags) scored per community by
`share × log(1 + lift)` — `share` alone selects vacuous tags every card carries
(`triggered-ability`), `lift` alone selects freak tags on 4 cards. Requires ≥4 cards per tag.

```
  5   522 cards   synergy-equipment / quick-attach
  6   489 cards   typal-goblin / burn
  7   437 cards   lands-matter / landfall
100    43 cards   synergy-desert / utility-land      (lift 327, recall 77%)
160    18 cards   typal-villain                      (lift 311)
```

Chosen over the user's initial suggestion of TF-IDF over type line / mana cost / stats because
`card_tags` already encodes mechanics rather than surface features.

**The failure mode is the valuable part.** A community whose best tag has lift < 3 is labelled
`(generic — no distinctive tag)`, and that fires on exactly the staple piles: 5 of the 12 largest
communities, 29 of 214 overall. It is an automatic detector for the low-quality communities.

## Interesting findings

**Community 160 is a precon artifact, cleanly demonstrated.** 18 cards, **16 of them in one precon**
(*Doom Prevails*, Marvel Super Heroes); sets 11 `msc` / 7 `msh`; EDHREC ranks 9,700–16,000. The
exception is Lethal Scheme at rank 1,327 — a genuinely popular convoke removal spell *reprinted*
into that precon, whose co-occurrence signal is dominated by intact precon shells. Its real identity
as generic removal is lost, and because Louvain is a hard partition it exists nowhere else.
`precon_card_weight` already exists in `cooccurrence.py` and is evidently too weak at 16/18.

**t-score is a significance measure, not an effect size — this is the root cause of three separate
complaints.** Lion Sash's 18 strongest edges span t-scores of only 2.98–4.41 while lift spans 1.2–62:

| card | t-score | lift | in decks |
|---|---|---|---|
| King of the Pride | 3.73 | **61.9** | 38 |
| Regal Caracal | 3.86 | **48.4** | 54 |
| Puresteel Paladin | 4.41 | **16.1** | 308 |
| Path to Exile | 4.16 | **3.06** | 2,865 |
| Swords to Plowshares | 3.81 | **2.54** | 3,866 |
| Arcane Signet | 3.62 | **1.24** | 8,682 |

Arcane Signet ranks 9th of 18 by t-score and last by lift. `(obs − exp)/√obs` means a tiny
*relative* excess on a large count is many sigma, so generic staples tie with genuine synergy
pieces. Independent confirmation from the naming system: both staples above sit in communities the
namer flagged `(generic — no distinctive tag)`.

**The "gravity pull" is mostly a low-degree tail, not gravity.** Community 7 (landfall) spans
strength 2,342 (Evolving Wilds) to 1.2 (Guiding Bolt) with a median of 32; community 100 (deserts)
spans 353 to 1.4. The apparently-irrelevant members — Guiding Bolt, Silverflame Ritual, Disciple of
Deceit, Desert Cerodon — have essentially one weak edge in the whole graph (EDHREC ranks
12,000–21,000). Louvain parks them wherever their single edge points because they have nowhere else
to go. Not all such cases are wrong: Miren, the Moaning Well (strength 29.3) is a legendary land
that genuinely co-occurs with utility-land decks.

**Cross-colour edges clear a higher bar than same-colour ones.** Colour identity restricts legality,
so a green–blue edge can only arise from the smaller set of decks legally able to run both. The
colour-conditioned null already accounts for this, so surviving cross-colour edges mean "above
chance among decks that could run both" — which is why they tend to be the mechanically meaningful
ones and why the residual ~53% purity is largely honest.

## Impediments

- **The weighting regression** (above) — cost a full rebuild and re-tune of γ, found by eye, not by
  test. Root cause is an API gap: `build_and_save` cannot pass weights even if a caller wants to.
- **First domination metric was vacuous** and reported "95% dominated" for every build including
  good ones. Rewritten around the ≥50%-of-slot-decks signature definition.
- **`deck_cards` has no index on `oracle_id`.** The first `neighbors()` helper issued 2 queries per
  neighbour → ~31 full scans of 1.06M rows, taking minutes for one interactive call. Rewritten to
  read `deck_count` from `card_index` and fetch all co-occurrences in a single self-join: 0.10s.
  Adding the index would be the more general fix and has not been done.
- Nested triple-quoted docstrings inside the notebook-builder's `r"""..."""` cell literals are a
  syntax error; cells containing docstrings use `r'''...'''`.
- ipysigma has no built-in edge-weight slider. Thresholding is done in Python and the graph
  re-rendered.

## Notes for future work

Three open quality problems, in priority order:

1. **t-score conflates significance with effect size** (the Lion Sash finding). Proposed fix: a
   two-gate sparsification — keep an edge only if `t-score ≥ ~2` **and** `lift ≥ L`. t-score stays
   as the reliability gate (this is why PMI was rejected in 6.1b — it ties rare coincidences at a
   ceiling); lift becomes the effect-size gate. Both already exist in `cooccurrence.py`.
   **Unresolved tension:** for Scute Swarm, Evolving Wilds (lift 2.56) and Fabled Passage (4.05) are
   *genuine* landfall payoffs that are also played everywhere. L=5 cuts them; L=3 keeps Path to
   Exile. There may be no single threshold that is right for both, which would argue for using lift
   as the edge *weight* rather than as a hard gate. Not implemented — the choice is the user's.
2. **Low-degree tail pollutes communities.** Cards with strength ≈1–3 have one weak edge and are
   assigned essentially arbitrarily. A membership floor (minimum strength, or minimum edge count
   *within* the assigned community) is independent of (1) and much simpler.
3. **Precon shells survive `precon_card_weight`** — community 160 is 16/18 one precon.

Also open, lower priority:

- **Hard partition loses genuine multi-membership** (Lethal Scheme as both villain-typal and generic
  removal). This is the one thing the archived NMF approach did better. If it matters, the answer is
  probably an overlapping-community method, not a return to NMF.
- Communities auto-named `(generic — no distinctive tag)` — 29 of 214, concentrated among the
  largest — are the natural targets for splitting.
- `edhcut/analysis/{nmf,symnmf_packages,symnmf_hierarchy,theme_labels}.py` and their tests are now
  unused. See `docs/devlog/archive/README.md`; deletion not done pending a call on the final report.
