## The edge-quality arc

### Gate 2: neighbourhood overlap (Jaccard), threshold 0.03

**The problem.** t-score is a *significance* measure, `(obs−exp)/√obs`, so on a large count a tiny
relative excess is many sigma. On Lion Sash's edge list Arcane Signet (8,682 decks) outranked
Hammer of Nazahn. The user's requirement was concrete: keep Evolving Wilds↔Scute Swarm, drop Path
to Exile↔Lion Sash.

**Lift cannot do this, as a gate or as a weight — proven, not assumed.** Pairs that must survive
span lift 2.56–61.94; pairs that must not span 1.24–3.06. They *overlap*, and in the wrong
direction (Evolving Wilds 2.56 keep, Path to Exile 3.06 drop). A weight is monotone in the same
quantity, so it only rescales an ordering that is already wrong. **Nothing computable from
`(co-occurrence, count_A, count_B, N)` can separate them.**

**What works: Jaccard overlap of the two cards' neighbour sets.** Keep-pairs 0.042–0.375,
drop-pairs 0.002–0.012 — a clean 3.5× gap. Scute Swarm and Evolving Wilds share 67 neighbours;
Lion Sash and Path to Exile share 7. Validated across all 224k edges: at 0.03, edges to cards in
3,000+ decks are cut to **1.6%** while edges to cards in <500 decks keep **85.1%**.

The gate is per *edge*: Evolving Wilds loses most of its edges but keeps the Scute Swarm one.

Raised 0.02 → 0.03 after review. Everything improved at once: Q 0.593 → 0.714, largest community
757 → 489, package-sized 113 → 140. The 0.02–0.03 band was inspected before committing and is
overwhelmingly staple-to-staple noise (Farseek↔Sol Ring, Command Tower↔Beast Within), **82% of it
cross-community**.

### The membership floor: k-core, k=4

Louvain assigns every node somewhere, so a card with one weak edge gets filed wherever it points.
Fixed with the **k-core of each community's induced subgraph**, iterated to a fixed point. `k=4`
chosen against a hand-labelled set: removes **11 of 12** cards flagged wrong on inspection,
**0 of 12** known core members. Dropped cards are marked `-1`, matching `communities.py`'s
convention.

symNMF needed one too, and **the obvious signal fails**: a card with one weak edge puts all its
tiny membership mass in one place and reads as **100% committed on nothing** (Mishra's Research
Desk: 100% share, strength 0.1) while Impact Tremors is 54% because it genuinely spans themes.
Thresholding on share keeps the junk and drops the real cards. The soft floor k-cores each topic's
membership set instead, so a card can survive in some topics and be peeled from others — 14% of
cards dropped versus 26% for a primary-only variant.

**Cost, measured:** the strongest casualties are *connector* cards — Prismatic Vista (strength
98.5, 454 decks, EDHREC rank 197, but only 17% top share across 4 topics), Command Beacon, Savage
Lands. Spread across several topics, they never reach 4 internal edges in any one.

### Edge weight: `t-score × log(1 + lift)`

t-score alone ranks fetchlands above the ABU duals next to Underground Sea (1 of 6 duals in the
top 10); the combined weight gets **6 of 6**. Formula chosen by testing, not assertion — a first
pass used `√(t·lift)` purely by convention and it tested *worst* of the combined forms.

**Methodological caveat, recorded because it inverts a conclusion:** two of the three benchmark
sets (Lion Sash, Scute Swarm) were built by reading each card's existing top *t-score* neighbours,
which rigs the comparison in t-score's favour. Only the duals case is unbiased, because the ten
duals are defined by the game. So the apparent *cost* of blending lift is largely an artifact; a
fair re-test needs ground truth from `card_tags` typal groups.

### Format-staple exclusion

**The confound the user named**: Sol Ring, Command Tower and Arcane Signet co-occur because the
same *builders* choose them, not because they interact. This is a latent **deck-level preference**
variable, structurally identical to the colour-legality confound task 6.1 already solved.

**The Jaccard gate cannot touch it** — checked live, **45 of 45 staple↔staple edges survive**, and
Sol Ring↔Arcane Signet at **0.786** is the highest-overlap pair in the graph. The gate asks "do
these keep the same company"; two cards in 66–83% of all decks keep exactly the same company. The
gate worked correctly; the confound is elsewhere.

**The detector: `max_lift`** — a card's strongest affinity with anything at all.

| card | play rate | max lift |
|---|---|---|
| Sol Ring | 82.5% | **1.13** |
| Command Tower | 66.9% | 1.46 |
| Swords to Plowshares | 29.3% | 2.86 |
| Evolving Wilds | 16.5% | **4.84** |
| Lion Sash | 0.8% | **104.6** |

A generic staple is *a card with no above-chance affinity to anything*. `max_lift < 3.5 AND
playrate ≥ 10%` selects exactly 12 cards — Sol Ring, Command Tower, Arcane Signet, Exotic Orchard,
Swords to Plowshares, Path to Exile, Swiftfoot Boots, Lightning Greaves, Reliquary Tower, Rogue's
Passage, Path of Ancestry, Fellwar Stone — and correctly **spares** Evolving Wilds (4.84).

Excluded as graph nodes on the same reasoning `communities.py` already uses for basic lands, now
measurable rather than asserted. `max_lift` must be measured on the **gated** graph; pre-gate it
finds only 6 of the 12. Basics are excluded from the test (being already excluded gives them
max_lift 0, double-counting them).

Decided **against** a staples community: per the user, these cards aren't universally correct
anyway (Exotic Orchard wants 3+ colours, Rogue's Passage wants voltron, Reliquary Tower wants
burst draw), so the label would say little. The future recommender will surface them via a
popularity component instead.

### Automatic naming

`card_tags` (Scryfall Tagger, ~377k rows, ~950 tags), scored `share × log(1+lift)`. A tag is the
theme only if it **both covers enough and is distinctive**: `share ≥ 10%` AND `lift ≥ 8` over ≥4
cards. Each half alone was tried and fails — `share` alone picks `triggered-ability` (55% of a
community at lift 1.1); `lift` alone picks freak tags (a 711-card community always has some tag at
lift ≥8 on 4 cards, which labelled it `power-matters-individual`).

Fallback when no tag qualifies, in place of a bad tag:
- **colour identity** — smallest set of ≤3 colours covering ≥85% of the community, where a card
  counts as covered when its identity is a *subset* (i.e. legal in a deck of those colours).
  Stricter and more meaningful than "which colour appears most": a community can be 44% blue and
  still not be blue. Rendered with the game's own names (Izzet, Jeskai, Temur).
- **power** — `cards.game_changer` density ≥8× baseline with ≥3 of them ⇒ `cEDH`. Sharp, not
  marginal: the cEDH community sits at 32× with median CMC 2.0 and median price $5.02 against
  ~$0.30 elsewhere.
- **kind** — staple density vs. the 4.0% graph baseline: ≥1.5× `goodstuff`, ≤0.5× `niche`, else
  `generic`.

## The soft-clustering arc

### k-clique percolation: tested and rejected

`networkx.k_clique_communities` **ignores edge weights entirely**, so it cannot tell a t-score 30
edge from a t-score 2 one. On this graph it percolates into a single **15,479-node blob** at k≤5,
and yields only **1.02–1.19 communities per node** — barely soft at all. k=6 is the first workable
setting and still leaves a 2,549-card blob. Cost was never the issue (1.3s for cliques).

### symNMF on the gated graph

`symnmf_packages.fit_symnmf` was built for 6.3-B and abandoned in the 2026-08-13 reset, but it had
only ever seen the **ungated** matrix (5.5M nonzeros, staple bleed intact). On the gated graph
(312k nonzeros) it behaves differently, which is what made re-running it worthwhile.

`S ≈ H·Hᵀ`, `H ≥ 0`, k=200, ~25s. Membership share per card, `MIN_SHARE = 0.10`, `MAX_TOPICS = 5`.

**What it does that Louvain cannot:**
```
Lion Sash → 39% Selesnya typal-cat/lifegain-matters   (Regal Caracal, King of the Pride)
          → 30% Boros quick-attach/quick-equip        (Sword of the Animist, Colossus Hammer)
          → 24% Boros equipment/quick-attach          (Sram, Puresteel Paladin, Open the Armory)
```
Scute Swarm resolves into five landfall *sub*-themes that the hard partition collapsed into one.

### Degree normalisation: tested and rejected by the user

symNMF minimises `‖S − HHᵀ‖²_F`, and the Frobenius norm is dominated by the **largest entries**.
Staples carry the biggest t-scores, so the optimiser spends whole components on them. Symptom,
measured: **the smallest topics held the most-played cards** — topics of ≤20 cards had median deck
count 136 against 21 for topics of ≥100, *inverted* versus Louvain (15 vs 25). Sol Ring, Arcane
Signet, Swiftfoot Boots and Lightning Greaves formed a private 7-card topic; the ten ABU duals
were split 7/3 across two.

`D^(-1/2) S D^(-1/2)` fixed all of that — median deck count for small topics 136 → 13, matching
Louvain's ordering; package-sized 80 → 101; duals reunited; staples merged into one 297-card pile.

**The user rejected it**: it produced hyper-specific small communities that weren't interesting and
made the large communities incoherent. Kept as a `NORMALIZE` toggle, default **False**, with the
comparison table in §2 so the trade stays visible.

Note the ABU dual split was purely a symNMF artifact — **all 10 duals are in one Louvain
community**, via transitivity (Underground Sea has no surviving edge to Savannah, Taiga or Plateau
— they share no colour — but they are linked through shared neighbours).

## Interesting findings

**Commanders are invisible to the graph.** `deck_cards` contains **0 of 13,207** decks' commanders.
Krenko leads 2,008 decks but his *node* is built from the 88 decks where someone ran him in the 99.
Isshin's node comes from 62 decks, Ziatora's from 43. Defensible for a cut-recommendation tool —
you never cut your commander — but it means commander cards have essentially junk nodes, and their
"very distinct synergies" are attributed entirely to the other 99 cards.

**Functional roles cannot emerge from co-occurrence, and shouldn't.** Boardwipes are *not*
substitutes (0 of 21 pairs below chance) but their mutual affinity is weak: median pairwise lift
2.79, versus 16.5 for landfall and 16.4 for elves. Since the graph keeps each card's top-15 edges,
a boardwipe's boardwipe-partners sit at ranks 94, 117, 135… of 504 and never enter the graph.
Starfall Invocation is homeless *because the gate worked*: its category-mates were eliminated at
sparsification, before either filter ran. "Boardwipe" is a **functional role** — plan task 6.4,
from `card_tags` — not a synergy package.

**A systematic 3-colour bias in the two new filters.** Controlling for popularity, in the 50–150
deck band: 0-colour 6%, 1-colour 5%, 2-colour 3%, **3-colour 31%** unassigned. Median edge Jaccard
0.043 for 3-colour cards against 0.076–0.078 for everything else, so the gate takes 30% of their
edges instead of 19%. Cause: a 3-colour card is legal only in decks whose commander covers all
three, and that pool spans many unrelated archetypes, so its partners spread thin.
`compute_color_conditioned_tscore` corrects the *null model* for legality, but **neither the
Jaccard gate nor the k-core floor knows anything about legality** — the correction stops at the
edge weights. An adaptive floor (guarantee each card its top-N edges by Jaccard) halves the bias
(31% → 16%) but rescued only 3 of 9 hand-picked cards and cost granularity; not adopted.

**Popularity must not be a clustering dimension.** The un-normalised symNMF fit *is* effectively
popularity-weighted, and it produced exactly the boutique staple topics the user rejected. That is
direct evidence on this data. Community membership is a property of *cards*; popularity matching is
a property of the *person*. Deferred to the recommender's ranking stage, where `deck_count` is
already available.

**`MIN_DECK_COUNT = 3` is the root of the remaining chaff.** Community 0's tail is genuinely weak
(Heart of Ramos: strength 0.38 against Goblin Matron's 768, surviving only because k=4 is low), but
community 41's is not: **Salt Marsh is in 5 decks and all 5 are Dimir zombie decks**, giving it a
mean lift to the zombie core of 61.6 — *higher* than Gravecrawler's 54.4. With 5 observations,
"part of the package" and "in the 5 decks we sampled" are indistinguishable. The median card in the
pool is in **14 decks**.

## Impediments

- **A `neighbors()` call took minutes.** `deck_cards` has no index on `oracle_id`, so a
  per-neighbour query meant ~31 full scans of 1.06M rows. Fixed by reading `deck_count` from
  `card_index` and batching co-occurrence into one self-join: **0.10s**.
- **The first staple-detection pass found 6 of 12** because `max_lift` was measured pre-gate, and
  separately mis-flagged the 5 basics (already excluded ⇒ max_lift 0).
- **`show_community` and `list_communities` disagreed** after `show_community` went soft by
  default — the former rendered 228 cards for topic 35, the latter reported 99. `list_communities`
  now prints both (`228 cards (99 primary)`).
- **Notebook-builder escaping**: `\n` inside f-strings collapsed to real newlines through the
  heredoc, producing unterminated f-string literals; and nested `"""` docstrings inside `r"""`
  cell literals are a syntax error (those cells use `r'''`).
- A side panel showing card images beside the graph was reverted at the user's request (took too
  much space). ipysigma's own info panel is text-only — `node_pictogram` takes Material icon names,
  not URLs — so there is no way to put the image in the graph itself.

## Notes for future work

1. **`MIN_DECK_COUNT = 3` is the highest-value remaining change.** Raising to ≥10 drops 38% of the
   pool (18,979 → 11,841) and would remove Salt Marsh, most of community 0's chaff, and much of the
   tail. Costs genuinely narrow cards. Thresholds measured: ≥5 → 15,995; ≥10 → 11,841; ≥20 → 7,801;
   ≥30 → 5,739.
2. **The 3-colour bias is unfixed** — the principled repair is to make the Jaccard gate and k-core
   floor legality-aware, mirroring what `compute_color_conditioned_tscore` does for the null model.
3. **Commander nodes are meaningless** (0/13,207 in `deck_cards`). If commander synergy matters to
   the recommender, it needs a separate mechanism.
4. **Precon shells still survive `precon_card_weight`** — community 160 in the pre-gate build was
   16/18 one Marvel precon.
5. **k selection for symNMF is unvalidated.** k=200 was picked to be comparable to Louvain's count.
   `sweep_k`/`best_k` exist but maximise seed stability, which is biased toward small k (this is how
   `nmf_packages` landed on the k=40 that was rejected as too coarse). 6.3-B's task-driven metric
   (`theme_labels.granularity_report`, resolved labels) peaked around **1,200 leaves** — a strong
   prior that k=200 is far too small. Cost scales as `n·k²`: k=1,200 is ~36× k=200's per-iteration
   cost, so ~30–60 min per fit.
6. **Rerun the formula comparison with unbiased ground truth** (`card_tags` typal groups) — the
   current one favours t-score by construction.
