# Task 6.3-D — Soft communities (symNMF) + the edge-quality rebuild
Date: 2026-08-14 | Status: done. **symNMF is the default going forward.**

Continues [6.3c-louvain-communities.md](6.3c-louvain-communities.md). Two entangled arcs: edge
quality (shared by both notebooks) and soft clustering (`notebooks/symNMF_communities.ipynb`).
Final graph: 16,026 nodes / 158,294 edges; symNMF gives 200 communities, 1.44 topics per card,
41% of cards in more than one.

## Decisions
- **Second sparsification gate: neighbourhood Jaccard ≥ 0.03.** The requirement was concrete —
  keep Evolving Wilds↔Scute Swarm, drop Path to Exile↔Lion Sash. **Lift cannot do this, as a gate
  or a weight, and that was proven rather than assumed**: keep-pairs span lift 2.56–61.94 and
  drop-pairs 1.24–3.06, overlapping *in the wrong direction*. Nothing computable from
  `(co-occurrence, count_A, count_B, N)` separates them. Jaccard does: keep-pairs 0.042–0.375 vs
  drop-pairs 0.002–0.012. At 0.03, edges to 3,000+-deck cards are cut to 1.6% while edges to
  <500-deck cards keep 85.1%. Raised from 0.02 after review — Q 0.593 → 0.714, largest community
  757 → 489, all at once.
- **Membership floor: k-core, k=4** on each community's induced subgraph, iterated to a fixed
  point. Removes 11 of 12 hand-flagged wrong cards and 0 of 12 known core members. For symNMF the
  obvious signal *fails* — a card with one weak edge reads as **100% committed on nothing** — so
  the soft floor k-cores each topic's membership set, letting a card survive in some topics and be
  peeled from others.
- **Edge weight `t-score × log(1+lift)`**: t-score alone ranks fetchlands above the ABU duals next
  to Underground Sea; the combined weight gets 6 of 6. Chosen by testing — a first pass used
  `√(t·lift)` purely by convention and tested worst.
- **12 format staples excluded as graph nodes**, detected rather than asserted. The confound is
  deck-level *builder preference*, structurally identical to the colour-legality one. The Jaccard
  gate cannot touch it — 45 of 45 staple↔staple edges survive, and Sol Ring↔Arcane Signet at 0.786
  is the highest-overlap pair in the graph, because two cards in 66–83% of decks genuinely keep
  the same company. The detector is `max_lift`: a generic staple is *a card with no above-chance
  affinity to anything*. `max_lift < 3.5 AND playrate ≥ 10%` selects exactly the right 12 and
  correctly spares Evolving Wilds (4.84).
- **Naming requires both coverage and distinctiveness** (`share ≥ 10%` AND `lift ≥ 8` over ≥4
  cards); each half alone was tried and fails. Fallbacks when nothing qualifies: colour identity
  (smallest ≤3-colour set covering ≥85% by *subset*, so a 44%-blue community isn't called blue),
  `game_changer` density ⇒ cEDH, then staple density ⇒ goodstuff/niche/generic.
- **Degree normalisation tested and rejected by the user.** Without it, the Frobenius objective
  spends components on staples — measurably, the *smallest* topics held the most-played cards
  (median deck count 136 vs 21), inverted versus Louvain. `D^(-1/2) S D^(-1/2)` fixed all of that,
  but produced hyper-specific small communities and incoherent large ones. Kept as a `NORMALIZE`
  toggle, default False, with the comparison table so the trade stays visible.
- **k-clique percolation rejected**: `networkx.k_clique_communities` ignores edge weights
  entirely, percolating into a single 15,479-node blob at k≤5 and yielding 1.02–1.19 communities
  per node — barely soft at all.

## Outcome
Done. symNMF does what Louvain structurally cannot — Lion Sash resolves 39% Selesnya cat/lifegain,
30% Boros quick-attach, 24% Boros equipment; Scute Swarm splits into five landfall sub-themes the
hard partition collapsed into one. Re-running symNMF was worthwhile precisely because it had only
ever seen the **ungated** matrix (5.5M nonzeros); on the gated graph (312k) it behaves differently.

### Findings worth carrying forward
- **Commanders are invisible to the graph.** `deck_cards` holds 0 of 13,207 decks' commanders, so
  Krenko's node is built from the 88 decks where someone ran him in the 99. Defensible for a cut
  recommender — you never cut your commander — but commander nodes are junk.
- **Functional roles cannot emerge from co-occurrence, and shouldn't.** Boardwipes aren't
  substitutes (0 of 21 pairs below chance) but their mutual affinity is weak — median pairwise
  lift 2.79 vs 16.5 for landfall. A boardwipe's category-mates sit at rank 94+ of 504 and never
  enter a top-15 graph. That's task 6.4's job, from `card_tags`.
- **Systematic 3-colour bias in both new filters**: controlling for popularity, 31% of 3-colour
  cards go unassigned against 3–6% for everything else. `compute_color_conditioned_tscore`
  corrects the *null model* for legality, but neither the Jaccard gate nor the k-core floor knows
  anything about legality — the correction stops at the edge weights. An adaptive floor halves the
  bias but rescued only 3 of 9 hand-picked cards; not adopted.
- **Popularity must not be a clustering dimension** — the un-normalised fit is effectively
  popularity-weighted and produced exactly the boutique staple topics the user rejected. Community
  membership is a property of *cards*; popularity matching is a property of the *person*. Deferred
  to the recommender's ranking stage.

### Open
1. **`MIN_DECK_COUNT = 3` is the highest-value remaining change.** Salt Marsh is in 5 decks, all 5
   Dimir zombie decks, giving it a higher mean lift to the zombie core than Gravecrawler — at 5
   observations, "part of the package" and "in the 5 decks we sampled" are indistinguishable. The
   median card in the pool is in 14 decks. Raising to ≥10 drops 38% of the pool.
2. The 3-colour bias is unfixed; the principled repair is legality-aware gating.
3. Commander nodes need a separate mechanism if commander synergy matters to the recommender.
4. Precon shells still survive `precon_card_weight`.
5. **symNMF's k=200 is unvalidated** — picked for comparability with Louvain. `sweep_k` maximises
   seed stability, which is biased toward small k (that's how `nmf_packages` landed on the k=40
   that was rejected as too coarse); 6.3-B's task-driven metric peaked around 1,200 leaves. Cost
   scales `n·k²`, so k=1,200 is ~36× per iteration.
6. **The edge-weight comparison favours t-score by construction** — two of three benchmark sets
   were built by reading each card's existing top *t-score* neighbours. Only the duals case is
   unbiased. A fair re-test needs ground truth from `card_tags` typal groups.
