# Task 6.1 — Co-occurrence & association scores
Date: 2026-08-02 (color-conditioned t-score added 2026-08-12) | Status: done

## Goal & hypothesis
First module of phase 2 (`edhcut/analysis/`): turn the raw `deck_cards` table (1,230 decks /
97,911 rows, from phase 1) into sparse card×card co-occurrence counts — global and per-
commander-slot — plus smoothed PMI and lift association scores, saved as reusable `.npz`/
`.parquet` artifacts under `data/kb/dev/`. Hypothesis: even this first, simplest signal
(co-occurrence alone, no embeddings/communities yet) should already surface recognizable
Magic associations for an eyeball check, and the two named §7 risks — precon contamination
inflating some slots' counts, small-corpus noise for thin slots like Orysa — should be
visible and need real mitigation, not just theoretical ones.

## Data
No new harvesting — pure derivation from the existing `data/edhcut.db`. Card pool ("cards
in >= 3 distinct decks", per the prompt): **3,095 of 31,622** total cards ever ingested by
task 5.2. `deck_cards` deliberately excludes commander(s) (task 5.3-B design) — so no
commander ever appears in this pool or any of its matrices; querying "Krenko, Mob Boss"
itself against its own slot's matrix correctly raises (not a bug, see Impediments).

Per-scope deck counts (all of phase 1's corpus, `slot_key`-partitioned): global 1,230,
krenko 301, kyler 300, yoshimaru_bruse_tarl 300, yenna 300, orysa 29 — matching 5.3-B/5.6's
already-recorded totals exactly.

## Method & design decisions
- **Card index**: `oracle_id`-ascending order (not deck-count order, which would tie-break
  arbitrarily) — one shared row numbering for the global matrix and every per-slot matrix,
  per plan §2.4's `card_index.parquet` contract. Saved once; every matrix is `.npz` scoped
  to the same `n_cards × n_cards` shape.
- **Precon-contamination down-weighting**: rather than the plan's example phrasing ("decks
  collectively count as a few, not hundreds" — implying explicit grouping/capping), used a
  continuous per-deck weight: full weight to `precon_similarity <= 0.9`, linear decay to a
  floor of 0.1 at similarity 1.0 (an exact precon copy). This needs no notion of "which decks
  are near-duplicates of *each other*" (only each deck's own similarity to whatever precon it
  matched), and degrades the same way the plan asked: if hundreds of decks ever did cluster
  near the ceiling, their collective contribution would be bounded near `0.1 * count`, not
  `1.0 * count`. **Checked against the real corpus before trusting this**: only **1 of 1,230**
  harvested decks exceeds the 0.9 threshold at all — a Kyler deck at 0.949 similarity, weight
  ≈0.556 (global `total_weight` = 1,229.6, i.e. exactly `1230 - 0.444`). The scheme is
  real and exercised, just not doing heavy lifting at today's corpus size — 5.6's QA report
  had already flagged the *median* Kyler precon-similarity at 0.19, well under this
  threshold, so the scheme's near-inertness here is consistent with what was already known,
  not a surprise.
- **Smoothed PMI + masking**: `log((joint + k) * N / ((marginal_i + k) * (marginal_j + k)))`,
  `k = 1.0`, pairs seen in fewer than `MIN_PAIR_COUNT = 3` distinct decks (raw, unweighted —
  literally "seen <3 times" per the prompt) masked to absent rather than computed. This alone
  turned out **not sufficient** — see Impediments for the discovery and the fix
  (a low-count discounting factor) that was needed on top.
- **Lift** (`joint * N / (marginal_i * marginal_j)`) is deliberately left *undiscounted* —
  a plain "how many times more than chance" ratio for eyeballing, distinct from PMI (the
  smoothed, discounted score later phase-2 steps will actually consume).
- **Slot labels**: friendly filenames (`krenko`, `kyler`, `yoshimaru_bruse_tarl`, `yenna`,
  `orysa`) derived from `CONFIG.commander_slots`, rather than raw oracle_id-based `slot_key`s
  — reused `_resolve_local_oracle_id`/`slot_key_for` from `edhcut/ingest/archidekt.py`
  (same cross-module reuse pattern `edhrec.py`/`precons.py` already established) to map
  configured commander names to the DB's actual `slot_key`s.
- **`CooccurrenceResult` keeps raw + weighted pair dicts, not just sparse matrices**: PMI/lift
  need both the raw count (for the min-pair-count mask) and the weighted count (for the
  probability estimate) per pair, keyed identically — building that from two independently
  round-tripped sparse matrices would just be reconstructing information already on hand.

## Interesting findings
- **The first real result was useless, and the reason why is a genuinely instructive
  finding**: before adding discounting, `Purphoros, God of the Forge`'s (krenko slot)
  top-10 by PMI was ten essentially random, unrelated cards (`Prismatic Vista`,
  `Price of Glory`, `Seize the Day`, `Dictate of the Twin Gods`, ...) all tied at
  *exactly* +0.492. Root cause: whenever a rare card (small marginal count, e.g. 3-30
  decks) happens to co-occur with the queried card in *every single* deck it appears in
  — easy by pure chance at that sample size within a >1,000-deck corpus — the smoothed-PMI
  formula algebraically reduces to a constant independent of that rare card's own count,
  so dozens of unrelated low-count coincidences tie for the ceiling score and drown out
  cards with genuinely large, non-coincidental joint counts. This is exactly the plan §7
  "small-corpus PMI noise" risk, just triggered by one *side*'s small count rather than the
  whole corpus being small — the existing `min_pair_count=3` floor doesn't touch it at all,
  since 3 clears the floor easily. Fixed with the standard Pantel/Lin discounting factor
  (`(joint/(joint+1)) * (min(marginal_i, marginal_j)/(min(...)+1))`, applied multiplicatively
  to the log-PMI value) — barely touches well-supported pairs (tested: <5% change for a
  50-deck pair) but shrinks low-count pairs toward zero regardless of how extreme their raw
  ratio is. After the fix, Purphoros's top-10 becomes `Price of Glory`, `Seize the Day`,
  `Mox Amber`, `Inventors' Fair`, `Chrome Mox`, `Mana Echoes`, `Prismatic Vista`,
  `Treasonous Ogre`, `Scalding Tarn` — a plausible fast-mana/ritual-adjacent aggro shell,
  not a tied coincidence list.
- The same before/after held for `Sol Ring` (global, present in 984/1,230 = 80% of decks —
  common but not universal): pre-fix top-10 was ten unrelated low-count coincidences at a
  shared +0.222 ceiling; post-fix it's `Seething Song`, `Wheel of Fortune`, `Mana Vault`,
  `Chrome Mox`, `Phyrexian Altar` — a coherent "fast mana / cEDH-adjacent staples" reading.

## Impediments
- **Commanders are structurally absent from every matrix**: `deck_cards` excludes the
  commander(s) by design (task 5.3-B), so `top_associated`/the CLI correctly `KeyError`s if
  asked to look up "Krenko, Mob Boss" itself against any scope — this surprised the first
  manual eyeball attempt before the cause was traced back to that earlier, already-documented
  decision. Not a bug in this task; just a consequence worth remembering before the next
  person tries the same lookup.
- The discounting-factor gap (above) was the real impediment this task hit — the initial,
  literal implementation of the plan's PMI formula passed all unit tests and produced
  syntactically valid matrices, but the live eyeball check (which the plan explicitly asks
  for as this task's own definition of done) caught that the *results* were not useful
  before shipping. Worth remembering generally: unit tests on formula mechanics don't
  substitute for the qualitative sanity check the plan calls for — this task's own devlog
  process caught what its own test suite didn't.
- `scipy` wasn't yet an installed dependency of the shared `venv` (only `numpy`/`pandas`/
  `pyarrow` were, transitively) — added `numpy`/`scipy` to `pyproject.toml` and
  `pip install -e .` picked them up cleanly, no conflicts.

## Addendum: color-conditioned t-score (2026-08-12)

**The bug**: `compute_tscore`'s pooled null model (`expected = marginal_i * marginal_j / N`,
`N` = every deck) has no notion of Commander's color-identity legality rule, so two unrelated
same-color cards read as associated purely because they share a legality-restricted pool, not
because of synergy. Surfaced during task 6.3's live notebook review (user noticed a bias toward
same-color cards in the NMF topics; the same root cause turned out to also affect this module's
own pooled t-score).

**The fix** (`compute_color_conditioned_tscore` + `build_card_color_identities` in
`cooccurrence.py`; `playrate.py`'s `build_deck_color_identities`/`build_color_identity_deck_counts`
gained an optional `slot_key` scope, reused rather than duplicated), mirroring `playrate.py`'s
own `eligible_deck_count`:
```
N_i   = decks eligible to run card i (color-identity superset of card i's own identity)
N_ij  = decks eligible to run BOTH i and j (superset of the UNION of their identities)
p_i   = marginal_i / N_i   -- this *is* playrate.py's own play_rate metric
expected' = N_ij * p_i * p_j
t_color = (joint - expected') / sqrt(joint)
```

**Self-validating property, not just an empirical finding**: whenever either card is colorless
(no identity restriction, `N_i` = every deck), the formula reduces algebraically to exactly the
pooled `compute_tscore` value — confirmed live at 0.0% change on every colorless-involving pair
tested. This proves the correction only ever fires where there's a real color-restriction gap
to correct, never as a blanket dampener across the board.

## Notes for future work
- Orysa's 29-deck corpus survives the `min_pair_count` floor at a *higher* rate than the
  larger slots (see Evaluation), which is itself a signal that the floor alone under-
  protects genuinely thin corpora — worth watching whether task 6.5's synergy_links (a
  cold-start signal independent of co-occurrence) needs to carry more weight than PMI
  specifically for that slot in task 7.3's cutter, rather than assuming PMI is equally
  trustworthy everywhere it's non-zero.
- The discounting factor's exponents/shape (currently the plain Pantel/Lin form, no extra
  tuning) aren't yet configurable via `edhcut/config.py` — fine for now since no downstream
  task depends on tuning them yet, but if task 6.3's community detection turns out sensitive
  to the exact PMI values (not just their ranking), consider exposing `k`/discount on/off as
  config rather than module constants.
- `cooccur_<scope>.npz` stores the *weighted* (novelty-adjusted) counts, not the raw ones —
  raw counts only exist transiently inside `CooccurrenceResult` during a build and aren't
  persisted. Fine today (nothing downstream needs them), but if a future QA pass wants to
  audit the down-weighting's effect after the fact without re-running the build, it would
  need to re-derive raw counts from `deck_cards` rather than reading them back from `kb/dev/`.
